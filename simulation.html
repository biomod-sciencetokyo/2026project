Overview

This page presents two distinct mechanical models: a finite-time 2D coarse-grained simulation that screens for a model-defined local pore-formation event, and a 3D constrained reduced free-energy optimization that generates a static deformation visualization. The 3D visualization is not a time-evolving simulation; neither model establishes complete membrane rupture or vesicle lysis. We also assess the thermodynamic feasibility of the HCR design for building DNA Bundles.

The 2D model is used as a computational screening system. Its GOAL is to examine how the THREE PROGRAMMABLE PARAMETERS—vesicle radius (R), DNA Bundle bending rigidity (BDNA), and linker rest length (l0)—shape DNA Bundle–membrane interactions. Because changing R also rescales the membrane discretization, Bundle length, anchor number, and other geometry-dependent quantities, radius-dependent trends should be interpreted as the response of the jointly scaled model system rather than as curvature alone.

Each trajectory is evolved as a finite-time overdamped Brownian coarse-grained calculation while the DNA Bundle approaches the membrane. The readout is the occurrence of a model-defined local pore-formation event: a local spring-extension threshold in the discrete membrane model. This event is used only as a pore-initiation proxy; the 2D model does not resolve physical pore growth, resealing, leakage, or vesicle lysis. Detailed equations, parameters, and procedure are in the Appendix.

Model-Defined Local Pore-Formation-Event Probability

Unlike the representative GIFs, each heatmap summarizes the model-defined local pore-formation-event probability across simulated trajectories for combinations of linker length and Bundle stiffness. Each map shows a different vesicle radius.

Across the current parameter space, high DNA Bundle stiffness was a common feature of conditions with elevated model-defined local pore-formation-event probabilities. In the heat maps obtained for the smaller-vesicle conditions, high-probability regions were predominantly observed for a stiff DNA Bundle combined with a short linker. In contrast, in the larger-vesicle conditions, the high-probability region shifted toward combinations of a stiff DNA Bundle and a longer linker.

We therefore describe the result as a radius-dependent crossover within the present model. Because changing R also changes Bundle length, anchor count, repulsion discretization, and friction, this is not an isolated curvature effect.

1.2 3D Reduced Free-Energy Model

For the R0 = 50 nm reference condition, a constrained reduced free-energy model optimizes indentation depth, Bundle bend radius, and deformation-pocket width using aggregate cholesterol adhesion, DNA Bundle bending, and an approximate membrane bending/tension penalty. A static parametric membrane indentation is then constructed from the optimized quantities and exported as a 3D visualization. It supports qualitative comparison of deformation and local curvature; it is not a dynamic membrane simulation or a full Helfrich shape minimization. Pore formation and rupture are outside the claims of this model. The separate GUV-scale extension is described in the Technical Appendix; optimization details are also available in the Appendix.

Modeling Objectives & Structural Scale

The GLB is a rotatable static visualization, not a time-evolving model. Its membrane geometry is constructed after optimization; local curvature is then calculated on that parametric mesh as H = |H| and clipped to 0.015–0.075 nm−1 for display, with a color scale of 0.018–0.065 nm−1. The colors therefore show a qualitative local-curvature distribution, not an optimized curvature, threshold exceedance, or mean-curvature result.

2. Design and Thermodynamic Validation of 6-Helix Bundle HCR Sequences

To achieve programmable and stimuli-responsive bundle assembly, we computationally designed and thermodynamically evaluated the DNA sequences for the 6-Helix Bundle HCR system.

2.1 Sequence Design Conditions

The DNA sequences for the 6-Helix Bundle HCR system (seeds S1-S6 and hairpins H1-H6) were computationally optimized using NUPACK to eliminate unintended leak reactions and structural defects. Strict physical constraints were applied during the design process:

  1. Complete elimination of ≥4-nt palindromes and homopolymers.
  2. Enforcement of sequence diversity, requiring all four nucleobases (A, T, G, C) to be present within any 7-nt window to prevent local GC-bias.
  3. Multi-tube optimization (max_size=2) to rigorously suppress homodimerization and non-specific crosstalk between domains.

2.2 Thermodynamic Feasibility of the HCR Cascade

To verify the structural integrity and reaction feasibility of the designed 6-Helix Bundle HCR system, thermodynamic simulations were conducted using NUPACK. The monomer fraction of each hairpin (H1–H6) in isolation (1 μM) was evaluated, demonstrating exceptional structural fidelity (>99.7% monomeric purity) with negligible homodimerization (≤0.21%). The thermodynamic driving force for the HCR cascade was assessed by calculating the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) for each sequential binding event initialized by the trigger strand (S2). As summarized in Table 1, every hybridization step exhibits a substantial negative free energy change (ΔG < 0), establishing a robust energetic cascade that guarantees the spontaneous and irreversible assembly of the 6-Helix Bundle.
