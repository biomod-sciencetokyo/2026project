Overview

This page presents two complementary mechanical models: a 2D coarse-grained simulation that screens for model-defined local pore-formation events, and a 3D reduced free-energy model that estimates representative membrane-deformation shapes. We also assess the thermodynamic feasibility of the HCR design for building DNA Bundles.

1. Membrane Deformation Models

1.1 MD-CG Simulation in 2D Environment

The 2D model is used as a computational screening system to identify practical design regions for three programmable parameters: vesicle radius (R), DNA Bundle bending rigidity (BDNA), and linker rest length (l0). Our goal is to estimate which parameter combinations are more likely to produce the model-defined local pore-formation event and thereby guide the design of DNA Bundles for membrane loading.

Each trajectory is evolved as a finite-time overdamped Brownian coarse-grained calculation while the DNA Bundle approaches the membrane, using a Brownian-dynamics framework [4]. The readout is the occurrence of a model-defined local pore-formation event: a local spring-extension threshold in the discrete membrane model. This event is used only as a pore-initiation proxy; the 2D model does not resolve physical pore growth, resealing, leakage, or vesicle lysis. Physical pore studies describe additional mechanisms beyond this model-defined criterion [16–22]. Detailed equations, parameters, and procedure are in the Appendix.

2D Comparison: Linker Length, Bundle Bending Rigidity, and Vesicle Size

Linker rest length l0 changes both linker mechanics and the membrane-capture range (26–44 nm across the documented sweep); its influence is not limited to flexibility or distance.

Key trends and interpretation

Across the present 2D parameter screen, high DNA Bundle bending rigidity was commonly associated with the model-defined local pore-formation event. For the small-vesicle condition (\(R=50\) nm), shorter linkers were more favorable. For the large-vesicle condition (\(R=200\) nm), longer linkers were more favorable.

For small vesicles, a short linker is expected to transmit the mechanical loading of a rigid DNA Bundle more directly to a localized membrane region. A longer linker can provide additional relative compliance and attenuate this localized loading. In contrast, for large vesicles, a longer linker increases the model-defined cholesterol–membrane capture range and can allow membrane coupling over a broader accessible region. These are model-level interpretations of the observed trends, not direct evidence of complete membrane rupture or lysis.

Representative trajectories visualize the contrasting membrane-loading behaviors underlying these design trends.

1.2 3D Reduced Free-Energy Model

Why a reduced 3D model?

The 2D CG-MD model compares how DNA Bundle and membrane conditions affect local interactions, but it does not show how the membrane wraps around a Bundle or how deformation spreads in three dimensions. Evaluating every condition with time-evolving 3D CG-MD would be computationally demanding for this project, so we use a 3D Reduced Free-Energy Model instead.

What the model calculates

For the R0 = 50 nm reference condition, the model estimates a candidate deformation shape from prescribed cholesterol-mediated adhesion, DNA Bundle bending, membrane bending, and membrane-tension terms [1–3, 5–11]. Cholesterol-modified DNA structures have been used for membrane binding and remodeling [12–15].

\[ F_{\mathrm{total}} = E_{\mathrm{adhesion}} + E_{\mathrm{DNA,bend}} + E_{\mathrm{membrane,bend}} + E_{\mathrm{membrane,tension}} \]

\(E_{\mathrm{adhesion}}\) represents cholesterol-mediated effective adhesion.

\(E_{\mathrm{DNA,bend}}\) is the energetic cost of bending the DNA Bundle.

\(E_{\mathrm{membrane,bend}}\) is the local membrane-curvature cost.

\(E_{\mathrm{membrane,tension}}\) is the cost of deforming a tension-bearing membrane.

The full mathematical formulation, numerical update rules, and model-specific assumptions are described in the 3D model details in the Technical Appendix.

Interactive 3D Deformation Visualization

The GLB and deformation views show local membrane deformation at the DNA Bundle contact region. Its shape reflects the competition between effective adhesion, Bundle flexibility, membrane bending elasticity, and membrane tension. The GLB can be rotated for viewing but is static, not a time-evolving simulation. Its color shows visualization-only local curvature, not a pore-formation test or rupture threshold.

Bundle Radius as a Geometric Design Parameter

DNA Bundle geometry is an important factor in three-dimensional membrane deformation. The Bundle radius determines the width of the membrane-contact region and the geometric extent over which the membrane must bend around the Bundle. In contrast, effective bending rigidity determines how strongly the Bundle itself resists bending. These two Bundle properties therefore affect deformation through different mechanisms.

A wider DNA Bundle changes the contact geometry and distributes membrane deformation across a broader region. This radius comparison is a qualitative geometric illustration, not a quantitative radius-sweep result or a prediction of pore formation. Bundle radius is a geometrically important input parameter to include in future parameter sweeps.
