Overview

This page presents computational studies of DNA-bundle-mediated membrane disruption and HCR reactions. We use 2D coarse-grained molecular dynamics and 3D thermodynamic simulations to examine how DNA Bundle rigidity, linker length, and vesicle size influence membrane deformation and model-defined Rupture. We also investigate the self-assembly and reaction feasibility of the HCR system designed to build DNA bundles.

1. Membrane Disruption

1.1 MD-CG Simulation in 2D Environment

To understand how DNA bundles deform and rupture extracellular vesicle (EV) membranes, we established a two-dimensional coarse-grained molecular dynamics (CG-MD) simulation. This cross-sectional model lets us examine the competition between DNA-bundle bending stiffness and lipid-membrane elasticity. The model represents groups of atoms as single beads and includes the DNA bundle, cholesterol anchors, flexible linkers, and the EV membrane lipid bilayer, enabling large-scale structural dynamics to be observed over extended timescales.

2.1 Membrane representation

The EV-like membrane is represented by a closed periodic ring of Nmem beads with initial radius R:

Nmem = int(240 R / 150 nm),    ri(0) = R(cos θi, sin θi)

The initial spacing of neighboring membrane beads is

r0,mem = 2R sin(π / Nmem).

This prescription yields an initial spacing of approximately 3.927 nm at both R = 150 and 200 nm. Thus, radius-dependent trends are not caused simply by a change in the initial membrane discretization length.

Each pair of intact neighboring beads is connected by a Hookean spring. If ℓi is the length of edge i, the model-defined membrane tension is

Ti = kmem(ℓi − r0,mem),    kmem = 150.

This term represents in-plane membrane extension in the discrete contour. A curvature-smoothing contribution based on the discrete second difference ri−1 − 2ri + ri+1 suppresses unphysical sharp kinks. Its coefficient is kbend,mem = 25kBT/r0,mem. The term is inspired by Helfrich-type membrane elasticity, but it is not the exact variational force of a three-dimensional Helfrich surface.

While the membrane remains intact, an additional pressure-like restoring force constrains the enclosed planar area relative to A0 = πR2, with karea = 160. This term stabilizes the two-dimensional ring against global area drift. It is a planar enclosed-area constraint, not a three-dimensional volume, osmotic-pressure, or area-per-lipid constraint. Once a membrane spring undergoes Rupture, this area-restoring term is removed in subsequent updates.

2.2 DNA Bundle geometry and bending mechanics

The simulation contains one DNA Bundle of length

LBundle = 3R.

The DNA Bundle is represented by five connected constant-curvature arcs. Its generalized-coordinate vector is

q = (X, Y, θ, κ0, κ1, κ2, κ3, κ4)T,

where (X, Y) is the bundle position, θ is the overall orientation, and κm are the local curvatures. The construction enforces continuous position and tangent direction at segment junctions, allowing the DNA Bundle to bend smoothly without treating it as freely hinged rods.

The DNA Bundle is treated as a 12-chain assembly with effective radius

rDNA = 1.25√12 = 4.330 nm.

Its effective persistence length and bending modulus depend on the dimensionless internal cross-linking-efficiency parameter α. For the 2D comparison below, rigidity is reported as the effective DNA Bundle bending rigidity, BDNA:

Lp,eff = 2Nchains + 120(Nchainsα)3,    BDNA = Lp,effkBT.

Here Nchains = 12. Increasing the internal parameter increases the bending modulus, so a DNA Bundle with higher BDNA resists conforming to the membrane. The persistence-length/bending-modulus relation follows the usual worm-like-chain idea, whereas the cubic dependence on the internal parameter is a phenomenological rule used to model the sharp stiffening expected when inter-chain sliding is suppressed. In this 12-chain model, the higher comparison value corresponds to α = 0.9, and the lower value corresponds to α = 0.1. This mapping should not be presented as a directly measured constitutive law.

2.3 Cholesterol anchors, linkers, and membrane capture

Cholesterol anchors are placed every 15 base pairs (5.1 nm) along the DNA Bundle:

Nanchor = int(LBundle/5.1) + 1.

The number of anchors is therefore 89 at R = 150 nm and 118 at R = 200 nm. For an anchor–cholesterol separation d, the linker force is represented by a piecewise-linear, strain-stiffening law:

d < l0 Flink(d) = 3(d − l0)
l0 ≤ d < l0 + 6 Flink(d) = 10(d − l0)
d ≥ l0 + 6 Flink(d) = 60 + 18[d − (l0 + 6)]

The linker is therefore softer in compression, stiffer under ordinary extension, and still stiffer after exceeding the nominal contour length l0 + 6 nm. This form is an effective, numerically stable spring law; it is not an exact WLC or FENE linker potential.

A cholesterol particle becomes mechanically coupled to the nearest membrane bead when its separation satisfies

rmc < 24 + l0 nm.

The membrane reaction is applied in the local normal direction and distributed over five beads with weights 0.40, 0.20, 0.20, 0.10, and 0.10. A key consequence is that l0 alters two aspects of the model at the same time: it changes the linker rest length and expands the membrane-capture range from 26 to 44 nm across the present sweep. Thus, any apparent linker-length effect must be interpreted as the effect of this coupled design variable.

To avoid unphysical overlap, the DNA Bundle experiences a normal harmonic repulsion when points along its centerline approach the membrane more closely than rDNA + 1 nm. The number of evaluation points is

Nrep = max{15, int(LBundle/3)}.

This count increases from 150 at R = 150 nm to 200 at R = 200 nm. Because point forces are summed without an explicit arc-length weight, this parameter is both a numerical-resolution setting and part of the discrete representation of the total repulsive interaction.

2.4 Dynamical update

Membrane beads and captured cholesterol particles are propagated by an overdamped Brownian update:

Δr = (F/γ)Δt + √(2kBTΔt/γ) ξ.

Here kBT = 4.11 in the code energy units, γmem = 1.0, and γchol = 0.2. The DNA Bundle is not advanced by an additional explicit translation or rotation step. Instead, its eight generalized coordinates are updated by a semi-implicit 8 × 8 linear system including the linker tangent stiffness and DNA Bundle bending stiffness. The translational and rotational frictions are

γtrans = 1.5(LBundle/100),    γrot = LBundle2/12.

The simulation time step is Δt = 10−4 code-time units. Because the friction coefficients have not been calibrated to an experimentally specified viscosity, code time must not be converted directly into seconds.

3. Parameter sweep and Rupture definition
3.1 Sweep protocol

The current aligned sweep examines R = 150 and 200 nm, eight internal rigidity-parameter values spanning 0.1 to 1.0 (reported as effective BDNA), and eight values of l0 from 2 to 20 nm. Each parameter cell contains 50 stochastic trajectories. A trajectory contains 100 frames, each composed of 100 substeps, giving a fixed observation window for all cells. The result is the fraction of the 50 trajectories in which a first membrane-spring Rupture occurs during that window.

The single-trajectory GIF code accepts the same radius, internal rigidity setting, linker rest length, seed, number of frames, substeps, and time step through its settings, allowing a representative GIF to be generated for a selected probability-map cell. Before the first Rupture, the single-run and sweep implementations have been confirmed to yield identical states for validation conditions.

3.2 Operational definition of membrane Rupture

At the start of each substep, all intact membrane springs are examined. A membrane Rupture event is registered as soon as one spring satisfies

Ti > 150.

Since kmem = 150, this corresponds to an extension greater than 1.0 nm, or approximately 25.5% of the initial spring spacing. The threshold 150 is a force-like quantity in the code's discrete-spring model. It is not directly a physical membrane surface tension in mN m−1, and it should not be labelled as a calibrated pN value without an explicit unit-mapping procedure.

The current criterion is instantaneous and local: one sufficiently extended membrane spring anywhere on the ring causes Rupture. It contains no time-persistence condition, contact-zone restriction, spatial averaging, or area-per-lipid criterion. The reported maps should therefore be described as probabilities of model-defined first membrane-spring Rupture, not as direct predictions of pore nucleation, pore growth, or complete EV lysis.

For the small-vesicle condition, short linkers tend to promote more localized membrane loading when the DNA Bundle is sufficiently rigid.

For the large-vesicle condition, longer linkers can better preserve membrane-reaching coupling, shifting the favorable linker-length range.

These results describe the tendency for a model-defined local membrane opening, rather than complete membrane rupture or vesicle lysis.

Rigidity, Linker Length

Across the current parameter space, high DNA Bundle stiffness was a common feature of conditions with elevated membrane Rupture probability. In the heat maps obtained for the smaller-vesicle conditions, high-Rupture regions were predominantly observed for a stiff DNA Bundle combined with a short linker. In contrast, in the larger-vesicle conditions, the high-Rupture region shifted toward combinations of a stiff DNA Bundle and a longer linker.

We therefore describe the result as a radius-dependent crossover within the present model, rather than as a universal property of biological membranes.

1.2 3D Thermodynamic Simulation

To accurately investigate the conditions required for membrane disruption, we performed a 3D thermodynamic stabilization calculation. The primary reason for adopting this approach is that it allows us to visualize the thermodynamically stable positions of the DNA bundle and the liposome in a static system, enabling detailed observation of the stereoscopic deformation process. Furthermore, by establishing a critical curvature limit, we can effectively determine whether the membrane will rupture, making it a powerful method for exploring the exact conditions of membrane disruption. In our modeling, we established a system that finds the equilibrium state by minimizing the total free energy, which consists of the membrane's bending and tension energies, the DNA's bending energy, and the adhesion energy from the cholesterol anchors.

Details of the 3D Thermodynamic Simulation Equations and Parameter Settings

This simulation models the search for a thermodynamic equilibrium state that minimizes the total free energy of the system, Ftotal. Ftotal is defined as the sum of three energy terms:

Ftotal = Eadhesion + Edna_bend + Emembrane

The calculations are based on the thermal energy at room temperature (300 K), kBT ≈ 4.11 × 10−21 J.

Cholesterol Adhesion Energy (Eadhesion)

The adhesion energy per cholesterol molecule due to hydrophobic insertion, εchol, was set to 4.5 kBT (Baumgart et al., Nature, 2003). For a DNA bundle of length Lbundle = 60 nm modified with Nchol = 22 cholesterols, the total theoretical adhesion energy is:

Eadh,total = 22 × 4.5 kBT = 99 kBT

The simulation calculates the actual energy gained proportionally to the contact area, which depends on the membrane indentation depth (zindent) and the bundle radius (rdna).

DNA Bundle Bending Energy (Edna_bend)

The DNA bundle is treated as an elastic beam based on Euler-Bernoulli beam theory. The energy cost for a bundle with rigidity Bdna to bend at a radius of curvature Rbend is:

Edna_bend = ½ Bdna (1/Rbend)2 Lbundle

Membrane Deformation Energy (Emembrane)

Based on Helfrich theory, we calculate the deformation cost of the target liposome with an initial radius R0 = 50 nm. We used a membrane bending rigidity κm = 20 kBT (Rawicz et al., Biophysical Journal, 2000) and a membrane fluctuation tension γm = 1.0 × 10−4 mN/m. The bending and tension penalties are calculated using the effective deformation depth (zeff), which accounts for the geometric mismatch between the straight DNA rod and the spherical liposome, and the width of the membrane deformation pocket (wy).

Using SciPy's L-BFGS-B optimization algorithm, we simultaneously optimize these three variables— zindent, Rbend, and wy— to find the geometric solution where Ftotal is minimized.

Modeling Objectives & Structural Scale

The model finds the thermodynamically stable configuration by minimizing the total free energy contributed by membrane bending and tension, DNA bending, and cholesterol-anchor adhesion. Establishing a critical average curvature of 0.067 nm-1 provides a threshold for determining whether the membrane is predicted to rupture.

By using the L-BFGS-B optimization algorithm to solve for the equilibrium state where the membrane's elastic energy (based on Helfrich theory) competes with the DNA's bending energy (based on Euler-Bernoulli beam theory), we constructed 3D curvature heatmaps. This spatially proved that when a highly rigid, straight DNA bundle adheres to a liposome, the membrane is sharply folded at the bundle's edges, causing extreme stress concentration.

Subsequent parameter exploration revealed that not only the "bending rigidity" but also the "bundle radius (thickness)" is crucial for membrane rupture. While increased rigidity severely distorts the membrane, a thicker bundle spreads the stress laterally due to a wider contact area, thereby reducing local curvature. Since both of these physical scalings depend directly on the number of bundled DNA strands (N), we reasoned that we could theoretically calculate the minimum number of strands required to disrupt the membrane.

Minimum Bundle Requirements & Cross-linking Efficiency

We performed a 2D scaling analysis, plotting the number of strands (N) on the X-axis against the maximum membrane curvature (Hmax) on the Y-axis. Assuming an ideal rigid DNA bundle (cross-linking efficiency α = 1.0), the bundle's radius is calculated as r ≈ 1.25√N, and its bending rigidity increases proportionally to the square of the number of strands, B ∝ α · N2. When these parameters are fed back into our 3D stabilization simulation, the curvature initially spikes due to the explosive increase in rigidity, but eventually, the stress dispersion from the increasing radius takes over, creating a "convex saturation curve." In this calculation, the curve breached the critical membrane rupture threshold (0.067 nm−1) at approximately 6 DNA helices. This quantitatively demonstrates that under ideal, perfectly cross-linked conditions, a minimum of 6 DNA strands is required to initiate membrane disruption.

2. Design and Thermodynamic Validation of 6-Helix Bundle HCR Sequences

To achieve programmable and stimuli-responsive bundle assembly, we computationally designed and thermodynamically evaluated the DNA sequences for the 6-Helix Bundle HCR system.

2.1 Sequence Design Conditions

The DNA sequences for the 6-Helix Bundle HCR system (seeds S1-S6 and hairpins H1-H6) were computationally optimized using NUPACK to eliminate unintended leak reactions and structural defects. Strict physical constraints were applied during the design process:

  1. Complete elimination of ≥4-nt palindromes and homopolymers.
  2. Enforcement of sequence diversity, requiring all four nucleobases (A, T, G, C) to be present within any 7-nt window to prevent local GC-bias.
  3. Multi-tube optimization (max_size=2) to rigorously suppress homodimerization and non-specific crosstalk between domains.

2.2 Thermodynamic Feasibility of the HCR Cascade

To verify the structural integrity and reaction feasibility of the designed 6-Helix Bundle HCR system, thermodynamic simulations were conducted using NUPACK. The monomer fraction of each hairpin (H1–H6) in isolation (1 μM) was evaluated, demonstrating exceptional structural fidelity (>99.7% monomeric purity) with negligible homodimerization (≤0.21%). The thermodynamic driving force for the HCR cascade was assessed by calculating the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) for each sequential binding event initialized by the trigger strand (S2). As summarized in Table 1, every hybridization step exhibits a substantial negative free energy change (ΔG < 0), establishing a robust energetic cascade that guarantees the spontaneous and irreversible assembly of the 6-Helix Bundle.
