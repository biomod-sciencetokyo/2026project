1.1 2D CG Model: Mathematical Formulation

This section gives the equations, parameters, and numerical procedure of the 2D coarse-grained molecular dynamics model used for the parameter screen on the Simulation page. Source code and raw outputs are listed in the Source Code Inventory.

Geometry and state variables

The membrane is represented by bead positions \(\mathbf r_i\) on a closed periodic ring. The DNA Bundle state comprises translation, rotation, and five piecewise-constant curvatures:

\[ \mathbf q = (x_{\mathrm{DNA}},y_{\mathrm{DNA}},\theta, \kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3,\kappa_4,\kappa_5) \]

The ring discretization is defined by:

\[ N_{\mathrm{mem}} = \operatorname{int}\!\left(\frac{240R}{150}\right) \]
\[ r_{0,\mathrm{mem}} = 2R\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{N_{\mathrm{mem}}}\right) \]

The 2D DNA Bundle contains 12 chains and is represented by five connected constant-curvature arcs, with length \(L_{\mathrm{Bundle}}=3R\). Its effective bending rigidity is mapped from the crosslinking efficiency (\(\alpha\)) by:

\[ L_{\mathrm{p,eff}} = 2N_{\mathrm{chains}}+ 120\left(N_{\mathrm{chains}}\alpha\right)^3 \]
\[ B_{\mathrm{DNA}}=L_{\mathrm{p,eff}}k_{\mathrm B}T \]

In the 2D model, crosslinking efficiency (\(\alpha\)) enters only through this phenomenological rigidity mapping; its numerical scale is defined separately from \(\alpha\) in the 3D helix-count screening. DNA flexibility and the mechanics of DNA assemblies have been studied experimentally and computationally [8–11].

Membrane-force model

For an intact spring, let \(r_i=\lVert\mathbf r_{i+1}-\mathbf r_i\rVert\). Its local tension and force are:

\[ T_i=k_{\mathrm{mem}}\left(r_i-r_{0,\mathrm{mem}}\right) \]
\[ \mathbf F_i^{\mathrm{spring}}= T_i\frac{\mathbf r_{i+1}-\mathbf r_i}{r_i} \]

Contour smoothing uses the discrete second difference below to construct a discrete contour-smoothing force. This implementation-level force is not a complete continuous Helfrich energy; it is a discrete force inspired by the broader continuum descriptions of membrane elasticity [1, 2, 5–7].

\[ \mathbf C_i= \mathbf r_{i-1}-2\mathbf r_i+\mathbf r_{i+1} \]

The enclosed polygon area and relative area strain are:

\[ A=\frac12\left| \sum_i(x_i y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_i) \right| \]
\[ \epsilon_A=\frac{A-A_0}{A_0} \]

The area-restoring force is applied along the membrane normal \(\mathbf n_i\):

\[ \mathbf F_i^{\mathrm{area}}= -k_{\mathrm{area}}\epsilon_A r_{0,\mathrm{mem}}\mathbf n_i \]

Linker, cholesterol capture, and steric repulsion

For linker distance \(d\), rest length \(l_0\), and extension \(\Delta=d-l_0\), the current piecewise force magnitude is:

\[ f_{\mathrm{linker}}(\Delta)= \begin{cases} 3\Delta, & \Delta<0,\\ 10\Delta, & 0\leq\Delta<6,\\ 60+18(\Delta-6), & \Delta\geq6 \end{cases} \]
\[ \mathbf F_{\mathrm{linker}}= -f_{\mathrm{linker}}(\Delta) \frac{\mathbf r_{\mathrm{chol}}-\mathbf r_{\mathrm{anchor}}}{d} \]

This is a piecewise-linear strain-stiffening force law approximating compression, ordinary extension, and strong extension; it is code-defined and is not a WLC or FENE model.

Cholesterol–membrane capture acts when the nearest membrane point is within \(r_{\mathrm{mc}}<24+l_0\). Its normal force is distributed to the nearest bead and two beads on either side with code-defined weights \((0.10,\,0.20,\,0.40,\,0.20,\,0.10)\). Cholesterol-modified DNA nanostructures have been used for membrane binding [12, 13]; the cutoff and bead weights here are model-specific settings, not measured physical values.

DNA–membrane repulsion is a normal harmonic repulsion within the cutoff \(r_{\mathrm{cut}}=r_{\mathrm{DNA}}+1\) nm, where \(d\) is the nearest DNA-surface-to-membrane distance and \(\hat{\mathbf d}\) is its unit direction:

\[ \mathbf F_{\mathrm{rep}}= k_{\mathrm{rep}}(r_{\mathrm{cut}}-d)_+ \left[(\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n\right] \]

Numerical update

Membrane beads use an overdamped Brownian update, following the general Brownian-dynamics framework [4]:

\[ \mathbf r_i^{n+1}= \mathbf r_i^n+ \frac{\Delta t}{\gamma_{\mathrm{mem}}}\mathbf F_i^n+ \sqrt{\frac{2k_{\mathrm B}T\Delta t}{\gamma_{\mathrm{mem}}}} \boldsymbol{\xi}_i^n \]

The DNA Bundle is not advanced by explicit Euler. Its generalized-coordinate increment is obtained from a semi-implicit linear system:

\[ \left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\Gamma}}{\Delta t}+\mathbf H\right) \Delta\mathbf q=\mathbf f_q \]
\[ \mathbf q^{n+1}=\mathbf q^n+\Delta\mathbf q \]

In the current 2D sweep source, stochastic noise enters the membrane and cholesterol updates, while no direct Brownian-noise term is explicitly added to the DNA Bundle's generalized coordinates.

Model-defined local pore-formation event

The local event indicator is evaluated from intact membrane springs:

\[ \mathrm{event}= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\max_iT_i>T_{\mathrm{crit}}\right] \]
\[ T_{\mathrm{crit}}=150 \]

This is an operational threshold in the discrete model and is not calibrated to a physical pore-formation or rupture tension.

For \(n_{\mathrm{trial}}\) trajectories, the reported event fraction is:

\[ P_{\mathrm{event}}= \frac{1}{n_{\mathrm{trial}}} \sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{trial}}}\mathrm{event}_j \]

This is a model-defined local pore-formation event, not a direct prediction of physical membrane rupture, lysis, or complete pore growth. Physical pore formation and growth involve processes outside this operational criterion [16–22].

Changing \(R\) also changes membrane-bead count, DNA Bundle length, anchor count, and the number of DNA–membrane repulsion evaluation points. The current 2D sweep is therefore not a comparison in which radius alone is varied independently.

Numerical procedure and limits

One-run procedure
  1. Initialize a periodic membrane ring and one 12-chain DNA Bundle. The DNA Bundle uses five connected constant-curvature arcs and length LBundle = 3R.
  2. Apply the model's approach drive to bring the DNA Bundle toward the membrane.
  3. Evaluate membrane spring, contour-smoothing, area-restoring, linker, anchor-reaction, and DNA–membrane repulsion terms using the settings in Table S1.
  4. Advance membrane beads and captured cholesterol particles using the overdamped Brownian update. Update the DNA Bundle's eight generalized coordinates with the semi-implicit linear system.
  5. At each substep, inspect intact membrane springs. Register the first local event if any spring satisfies Ti > 150.
  6. After registration, the planar area-restoring term is removed in subsequent updates.
Raw data

Raw CSVs are provided for R = 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, and 300 nm. Each CSV contains 3,200 rows: 8 crosslinking-efficiency (α) / bending-rigidity values × 8 linker lengths × 50 trials. The files record `Alpha`, `B_dna(pN.nm^2)`, `Linker_Length_nm`, `Trial`, `Rupture_Success`, and `Rupture_Frame`; the `Rupture_*` columns denote the model-defined local pore-formation event. Each heatmap cell on the Simulation page is the percentage of the 50 trials in which the event occurred. The source scripts are listed in the Source Code Inventory.

  • R = 50 nm data · R = 100 nm · R = 150 nm
  • R = 200 nm data · R = 250 nm · R = 300 nm
Numerical checks and limits

The membrane discretization gives an initial spacing of approximately 3.927 nm. This is a geometric property of the discretization rule, not evidence of convergence.

Convergence and sensitivity with respect to time step, membrane segmentation, DNA–membrane repulsion evaluation-point count, and observation duration have not yet been evaluated and remain future validation.

1.2 3D Reduced Free-Energy Model

Model variables and free-energy functional

For the \(R_0=50\) nm reference condition, the optimization variables are indentation depth \(z_{\mathrm{indent}}\), DNA Bundle bend radius \(R_{\mathrm{bend}}\), and deformation pocket width \(w_y\). The reduced free energy combines aggregate cholesterol adhesion, DNA Bundle bending, membrane bending, and membrane tension. The membrane-elasticity and adhesion concepts provide physical context [1–3, 5–7]; this reduced functional is a model-specific approximation, not a full continuum surface model.

The contact geometry is defined by:

\[ \theta_{\mathrm{contact}}= \cos^{-1}\!\left(1-\frac{z_{\mathrm{indent}}}{r_{\mathrm{DNA}}}\right) \]
\[ A_{\mathrm{contact}}= N_{\mathrm{Bundle}}L_{\mathrm{Bundle}} \left(2r_{\mathrm{DNA}}\theta_{\mathrm{contact}}\right) \]

Let \(E_{\mathrm{adh,Bundle}}\) denote the positive aggregate adhesion scale per DNA Bundle. The adhesion contribution to free energy is:

\[ E_{\mathrm{adhesion}}= -\frac{E_{\mathrm{adh,Bundle}}} {L_{\mathrm{Bundle}}r_{\mathrm{DNA}}} A_{\mathrm{contact}} \]

DNA-nanostructure mechanics and membrane interactions have experimental and computational precedent [8–15], while the contact geometry and effective terms above are defined by this model.

\[ E_{\mathrm{DNA,bend}}= N_{\mathrm{Bundle}}\frac12B_{\mathrm{DNA}} \left(\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{bend}}}\right)^2 L_{\mathrm{Bundle}} \]
\[ \lambda_{\mathrm m}= \sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm m}}{\gamma_{\mathrm m}}} \]
\[ E_{\mathrm{membrane,bend}}= \frac12\kappa_{\mathrm m} \left(\frac{z_{\mathrm{indent}}}{w_y^2}\right)^2 A_{\mathrm{contact}} \]
\[ E_{\mathrm{membrane,tension}}= \frac12\gamma_{\mathrm m} \left(\frac{z_{\mathrm{indent}}}{w_y}\right)^2 A_{\mathrm{contact}} \left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm m}}{w_y}\right) \]
\[ F_{\mathrm{total}}= E_{\mathrm{adhesion}}+ E_{\mathrm{DNA,bend}}+ E_{\mathrm{membrane,bend}}+ E_{\mathrm{membrane,tension}} \]

Cholesterol positions and linker conformations are not individually resolved in 3D; adhesion enters as an aggregate term.

Constrained minimization and static shape construction

The three geometric variables are minimized using bounded L-BFGS-B:

\[ \underset{ z_{\mathrm{indent}},\,R_{\mathrm{bend}},\,w_y }{\operatorname{minimize}} \quad \frac{F_{\mathrm{total}}}{k_{\mathrm B}T} \]

The initial guess is \([4.0,\,50.0,\,6.0]\) nm, with bounds \(z_{\mathrm{indent}}\in[0.5,7.2]\) nm, \(R_{\mathrm{bend}}\in[35,250]\) nm, and \(w_y\in[3.2,12]\) nm. The reference solution is \([7.2,\,250,\,12]\) nm, at the upper bound for all three variables.

After optimization, a static parametric membrane surface and DNA Bundle shape are constructed and exported as a GLB visualization. This is not a time-evolving 3D MD or CG-MD simulation, and it is not a full Helfrich surface minimization.

The reduced model does not include:

  • area conservation or volume conservation;
  • osmotic pressure, lipid diffusion, or thermal fluctuations;
  • pore nucleation, pore growth, or membrane repair.

Because the optimized reference solution reaches all specified upper bounds, it indicates a bound-limited tendency within this ansatz, not a uniquely identified physical equilibrium.

Curvature visualization and limits

The GLB colors visualize local curvature calculated in post-processing from the parametric surface after optimization; curvature is not the minimized target. The calculation uses absolute curvature and clips displayed values to \(0.015\text{–}0.075\ \mathrm{nm}^{-1}\), with the color scale spanning \(0.018\text{–}0.065\ \mathrm{nm}^{-1}\). The color map does not identify pore formation, membrane rupture, or a critical-curvature crossing. Individual cholesterol positions and linker conformations are not resolved; cholesterol adhesion remains an aggregate term.

Curvature reference level for helix-count screening

The helix-count screen operationally uses \(H_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0.067\ \mathrm{nm}^{-1}\) as a model-defined comparison level. Its inverse, \(1/H_{\mathrm{crit}} \approx 15\ \mathrm{nm}\), is used only as a characteristic length scale within the screen’s inverse-radius convention.

This comparison level is physically motivated by two complementary observations. First, lipid-packing theory shows that strong curvature can generate composition-dependent packing frustration because the headgroup area, hydrophobic volume, and hydrocarbon-chain length cannot remain geometrically matched under severe bending [A1]. Second, micropipette experiments show that membrane failure depends on tension, area expansion, membrane composition, and loading rate rather than on a single universal curvature value [A2, A3].

Accordingly, \(H_{\mathrm{crit}}\) is used here to compare DNA Bundle helix-count and crosslinking-efficiency scenarios and to identify the model-defined \(N_{\mathrm{crit}}\). It is not presented as a universal lipid-bilayer failure constant, an experimentally calibrated pore-formation threshold, or a rupture trigger evaluated directly by the static 3D GLB model.

Phenomenological helix-count screening and crosslinking efficiency (α)

This is a separate screening analysis, not the output of the static 3D deformation optimization. Its effective-rigidity scaling is:

\[ B_{\mathrm{eff}}= \alpha C N^2L_{\mathrm{Bundle}} \]

The screening model defines a DNA Bundle curvature indicator and a maximum local-curvature indicator by:

\[ \kappa_{\mathrm{Bundle}}= \frac{k_{\mathrm{mem,eff}}} {B_{\mathrm{eff}}+k_{\mathrm{mem,eff}}} \frac{1}{R_0} \]
\[ H_{\mathrm{max}}= \frac{1}{R_0}+ 3\left(\frac{1}{R_0}-\kappa_{\mathrm{Bundle}}\right) \]

The crossing is defined by:

\[ N_{\mathrm{crit}}: \quad H_{\mathrm{max}}(N,\alpha)=H_{\mathrm{crit}} \]

Here, \(C=50\) is a model scaling constant and \(\alpha\) is the crosslinking efficiency (\(\alpha\)) of the helices within one DNA Bundle. The settings \(\alpha=1.0,\,0.5,\,0.1\) represent high, intermediate, and low crosslinking-efficiency scenarios. \(H_{\mathrm{crit}}\) is the comparison level described above, and \(N_{\mathrm{crit}}\) is a rigidity-based design estimate. Figures 13 and 14 use \(L_{\mathrm{Bundle}}=150\) nm, \(R_0=50\) nm, and \(k_{\mathrm{mem,eff}}=30000\); with these settings, \(\alpha=1.0,\,0.5,\,0.1\) give \(N_{\mathrm{crit}}\approx 3.8,\,5.4,\,12\). This screening formula does not include a physical radius function \(r_{\mathrm{Bundle}}(N)\), so it does not quantitatively combine radius-dependent geometry with helix-number-dependent rigidity. 1.4 Multi-Bundle Extension and Limitations

The 3D implementation can set the number of independent DNA Bundles with SIM_NUM_BUNDLES. In the current multi-Bundle extension, DNA Bundles are arranged as an independent parallel array and their free-energy contributions are added independently. Bundle–Bundle interactions, membrane-mediated cooperativity, and overlap-dependent mechanics are not yet included. This extension is not direct evidence that multiple DNA Bundles enhance membrane rupture.
