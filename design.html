Overview

Bundled HCR, including the seed and the Double-Hairpin DNAs, is our own design. The 12- and 13-Helix DNA Bundles follow previously reported designs [1, 2], which we chose for the membrane experiments and used with cholesterol modification.

Our simulations estimated that about 12 helices are needed to disrupt the membrane, while the growth mechanism of Bundled HCR can be tested with fewer helices. We therefore assigned these two roles to separate structures, which we designed and tested in parallel:

  • Isothermally grown 6-Helix DNA Bundle (6HB, Bundled HCR): a minimal design to verify that Bundled HCR proceeds from the seed (Experiment A).
  • Annealed 12- and 13-Helix DNA Bundles (12HB and 13HB): rigid, cholesterol-modified DNA Bundles built from established DNA tube designs [1, 2] to test anchoring and disruption of the membrane. The 12HB meets the estimated threshold, and the twisted 13HB, which carries cholesterol in all directions, serves as a comparison for the direction of cholesterol (Experiment B).

1. Bundled HCR

We have extended the concept of the Hybridization Chain Reaction (HCR) [3] and developed Bundled HCR. While conventional HCR yields a single long duplex DNA, Bundled HCR generates a long, thick DNA Bundle. Its components are a seed and six types of Double-Hairpin DNAs (H1–H6).

1.1 Seed and Double-Hairpin DNAs

As the materials for Bundled HCR, we prepared a seed and six Double-Hairpin DNAs (H1–H6).

1.2 Why Double-Hairpin DNAs

Like the 84-nt strands of the seed, each 84-nt Double-Hairpin DNA spans two neighboring helices. When it opens, it extends two helices at once and links them side by side, so that all helices grow together as one DNA Bundle instead of as separate duplexes. Each of its two binding regions is closed in a hairpin until the preceding strand opens it, so the hairpins do not react with each other in solution and the DNA Bundle grows only from an active seed.

1.3 Growth Cycle

Only in the presence of the Trigger DNA is the blocking strand displaced from the seed, switching it to an active state. The active seed then opens the Double-Hairpin DNAs by toehold-mediated strand displacement in a fixed order: H1, H2, and H3 open sequentially, followed by H4, H5, and H6, and the cycle then returns to H1, H2, and H3. Through this repeated cycle, multiple DNA duplexes elongate together as a DNA Bundle (Movie 1). By changing the seed design and the number of Double-Hairpin DNA types, DNA Bundles consisting of any number of helices can be assembled.

1.4 Sequence Design

The seed and hairpin sequences were designed with NUPACK under strict constraints: no palindromes of 4 nt or longer and no homopolymers, all four bases within any 7-nt window, and multi-tube design to suppress homodimerization and crosstalk (Simulation 2.1). Each designed hybridization step was predicted to be thermodynamically favorable (Simulation 2.2).

2. 12- and 13-Helix DNA Bundles

2.1 Why the 12HB and 13HB

12HB: our 3D free-energy model estimated that about 12 helices are needed to reach the target membrane-deformation condition (Simulation 1.2). We therefore adopted the 12HB as the rigid DNA Bundle for the membrane experiments.

13HB: the 13HB is the neighbouring structure in the same reported design series [1]. We prepared it as a comparison for the direction of cholesterol: the straight 12HB carries cholesterol on one side, whereas the twisted 13HB carries it in all directions (see 2.2).

2.2 Strands and Assembly

Following the designs reported in previous studies [1, 2], the 12-Helix DNA Bundle (12HB) and 13-Helix DNA Bundle (13HB) were formed by annealing the single-stranded DNAs V1–V12, T12, and T13 shown in Table 3.

In the 12-Helix DNA Bundle, V1–V11 and T12 hybridize as shown in Figure 3; in the 13-Helix DNA Bundle, V1–V12 and T13 hybridize as shown in Figure 4. Each assembles into a DNA Bundle hundreds to thousands of nanometers long.

Because cholesterol was attached to the 3′ end of the 42-nt strand V1, the resulting 12- and 13-Helix DNA Bundles carry a cholesterol modification every 21 nucleotides.

For the cholesterol-free 12- and 13-Helix DNA Bundles prepared as controls, unmodified V1 was used instead.
