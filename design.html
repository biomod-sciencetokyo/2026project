Overview

Our simulations estimated that about 12 helices are needed to disrupt the membrane, while the growth mechanism of Bundled HCR can be tested with fewer helices. We therefore split the roles at the design stage and designed two structures that we tested in parallel:

  • Isothermally grown 6-helix bundle (Bundled HCR): a minimal design to verify that Bundled HCR proceeds from the seed (Experiment A).
  • Annealed 12- and 13-helix bundles: rigid, cholesterol-modified bundles built from established DNA tube designs [1, 2] to test anchoring and disruption of the membrane. The 12-helix bundle meets the estimated threshold, and the 13-helix bundle is a higher-helix comparison (Experiment B).

1. Bundled HCR

We have extended the concept of the Hybridization Chain Reaction (HCR) [3] and developed Bundled HCR. While conventional HCR yields a single long duplex DNA, Bundled HCR generates a long, thick DNA bundle. Its components are a seed and six types of Double-Hairpin DNAs (H1–H6).

As the materials for Bundled HCR, we prepared a seed and six double-hairpin DNA strands (H1–H6).

1.2 Why Double-Hairpin DNAs

Like the 84-nt strands of the seed, each 84-nt Double-Hairpin DNA spans two neighbouring helices. When it opens, it extends two helices at once and links them side by side, so that all helices grow together as one bundle instead of as separate duplexes. Each of its two binding regions is closed in a hairpin until the preceding strand opens it, so the hairpins do not react with each other in solution and the bundle grows only from an active seed.

1.3 Growth Cycle

Only in the presence of the Trigger DNA is the Blocker strand displaced from the seed, switching it to an active state. The active seed then opens the Double-Hairpin DNAs by toehold-mediated strand displacement in a fixed order: H1, H2, and H3 open sequentially, followed by H4, H5, and H6, and the cycle then returns to H1, H2, and H3. Through this repeated cycle, multiple dsDNA strands elongate together as a bundle. By changing the seed design and the number of Double-Hairpin DNA types, bundles consisting of any number of strands can be assembled.

1.4 Sequence Design

The seed and hairpin sequences were designed with NUPACK under strict constraints: no palindromes of 4 nt or longer and no homopolymers, all four bases within any 7-nt window, and multi-tube design to suppress homodimerization and crosstalk (Simulation 2.1). Each designed hybridization step was predicted to be thermodynamically favourable (Simulation 2.2).

2. 12- and 13-Helix Bundles

Following the designs reported in previous studies [1, 2], the 12-helix and 13-helix bundles were synthesized by annealing the single-stranded DNAs V1–V12, T12, and T13 shown in Table 3.

In the 12-helix bundle, V1–V11 and T12 hybridize as shown in Figure 3; in the 13-helix bundle, V1–V12 and T13 hybridize as shown in Figure 4. Each assembles into a bundle hundreds to thousands of nanometers long.

Because cholesterol was attached to the 3′ end of the 42-nt strand V1, the resulting 12- and 13-helix bundles carry a cholesterol modification every 21 nucleotides.

For the cholesterol-free 12- and 13-helix bundles prepared as controls, unmodified V1 was used instead.
