Abstract

  1. 01

    Problem

    Extracellular vesicles (EVs) carry molecular cargo that is valuable for diagnosis, yet conventional disruption methods destroy origin information.

    Project →
  2. 02

    Approach

    This project uses a target-triggered Hybridization Chain Reaction (HCR) to assemble rigid DNA bundles on lipid membranes in situ, mechanically disrupting only the intended vesicles.

    Project →
  3. 03

    Simulation

    We use 2D coarse-grained molecular dynamics and 3D thermodynamic simulations to examine how DNA Bundle rigidity, linker length, and vesicle size influence membrane deformation and model-defined Rupture. We also investigate the self-assembly and reaction feasibility of the HCR system designed to build DNA bundles.

    Simulation →
  4. 04

    Experiment

    In Experiment A, we investigated the reaction mechanism of Bundled HCR using a minimal model designed to elongate into a 6-helix DNA bundle. In Experiment B, we evaluated the physical membrane-disrupting capability of cholesterol-modified 12-helix and 13-helix DNA bundles.

    Experiment →
  5. 05

    Future

    Potentially, our research may pave the way for molecular signal-responsive systems capable of lipid membrane disruption.

    By using cell-free cancer-associated miRNAs as sequence-specific triggers, such systems could eventually be extended from lipid vesicles toward larger membrane-bound targets, including living cells.

    Future →
